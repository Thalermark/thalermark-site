Thalermark is open source under the
[GNU AGPL v3](https://github.com/Thalermark/thalermark/blob/main/LICENSE).
Everything described in these docs lives in the
[public repository](https://github.com/Thalermark/thalermark), where anyone
can read it, run it, and audit every line.

## What AGPL means, in plain words

- You can **use, modify, and self-host** Thalermark, free, forever. For
  yourself, for clients, for a co-op, commercially. No seat counts, no
  feature gates.
- The one obligation: if you **modify** Thalermark and run your modified
  copy **as a service for other people**, you must offer those people your
  modified source code. Improvements to shared software stay shared.
- Running an unmodified copy, or a modified copy just for yourself, asks
  nothing of you.

That's a summary, not the license; the
[license text](https://github.com/Thalermark/thalermark/blob/main/LICENSE)
is the real thing.

## The commercial license

Some organizations want to embed Thalermark inside a closed-source product
(a proprietary client portal, a vertical SaaS, an appliance) where AGPL's
share-your-changes rule doesn't fit. For them, the same code is available
under a
[separate commercial license](https://github.com/Thalermark/thalermark/blob/main/LICENSE-COMMERCIAL.md).
Email [hello@thalermark.com](mailto:hello@thalermark.com) with what you're
building and we'll respond within five business days.

The community edition is not a teaser for that. It is the whole product,
AGPL, always.

## Contributing

Contributions are welcome:
[CONTRIBUTING.md](https://github.com/Thalermark/thalermark/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md)
covers the workflow. Before a first merge, contributors sign a lightweight
[CLA](https://github.com/Thalermark/thalermark/blob/main/CLA.md): you keep
the copyright to your work, and the project gains the right to ship it under
both licenses, which is what keeps dual-licensing legally sound.

## The name

"Thalermark" and the struck-mark logo are trademarks of the project. The
license covers the code, not the name: a fork is welcome, but it needs its
own name before it's distributed, and can't imply we endorse it.
