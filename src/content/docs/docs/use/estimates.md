An estimate is a quote: what the work will cost if they say yes. It doesn't
touch your numbers until it becomes an invoice.

## Create and send

**Estimates → + New estimate.** The form works exactly like
[an invoice](/docs/use/send-an-invoice/): same contact picker, same line
items and tax, with one difference. Instead of a due date there's an
optional **Expires** date. Leave it blank for an open-ended quote.

**Send estimate** emails the customer a link to their copy. You get a share
link too, for texting it over.

## The customer answers online

While the estimate is out, the customer's copy has two buttons: **Accept**
and **Decline**. Their answer shows up on your side immediately, and the
dashboard's **Open estimates** and **Accepted** tiles keep count.

Said yes on the phone instead? Use **Mark accepted** yourself. A quote that
died on the vine can be closed out with **Mark declined**. That's final, so
if they come back later, **Duplicate** it into a fresh one.

:::note[Expiry is a courtesy, not an ambush]
A quote past its expiry date shows "validity expired," but nothing slams
shut automatically. It's between you and the customer.
:::

## Turn a yes into an invoice

On an accepted estimate, click **Convert to invoice**. Every line carries
across into a new draft invoice, ready to
[send](/docs/use/send-an-invoice/). The estimate remembers it's been
converted, so you can't accidentally bill the same quote twice.

Sent the wrong price? **Fix this estimate** pulls it back the
[same way invoices work](/docs/use/fix-an-invoice/), and the customer can't
accept it until you resend the corrected version.
