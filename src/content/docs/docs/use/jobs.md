A job is whatever you'd call it out loud: "the Smith job," "Tuesdays at the
Chens'." Log hours against it, tag what you bought for it, bill it as many
times as it needs, and Thalermark tells you what it actually made.

Find **Jobs** in the account menu.

## Start a job

**+ New job** asks only what you call it, an optional customer, and dates if
you want them. That's it. A job doesn't need anything else to be useful.

## Log the hours

On the job's page, log hours with a date, the hours, an optional **rate**,
and what you did. Leave the rate blank for time you're not charging; it
still counts toward what the job cost you in time.

Prefer a timer? Open **Use a stopwatch**, hit **Start** when you begin and
**Stop** when you're done. Stopping fills in the hours for you to review
and log. Only one stopwatch runs at a time, so the same minute never gets
billed to two customers.

## Tag the costs

Every [expense](/docs/use/expenses/) has a **"What was this for?"** question.
Answer it with a job and that receipt counts against the job's numbers.
(Tagging changes nothing about your books or taxes; it's purely so the job
math is honest.)

## Bill it

**Bill this job** starts an [invoice](/docs/use/send-an-invoice/) with the
unbilled hours already on it as line items. A job can carry as many invoices
as it needs: a deposit and a final, or one every fortnight. Hours logged
without a rate are flagged **needs a rate** before they can be billed.

## The verdict

Five tiles across the top of every job:

**Ready to bill · Billed · What it cost · Made · Per hour**

"Made" is billed minus the costs you tagged; "Per hour" is what the job paid
for your logged time. Only you see these, never the customer. When the
work's done, **Close job** tidies it out of the default list (Thalermark
warns you first if money is still waiting to be billed).
